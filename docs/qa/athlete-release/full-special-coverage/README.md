# Complete special-tile display coverage

This is a local implementation and finite verification record. It is not a deployment, browser-pixel acceptance, phone FPS measurement, final integrated-venue clearance certificate, or new physical-contact certificate.

## Visible result and architecture

All 54 fixed special anchors display the original authored mechanism GLBs, independently of actor hashes, occupancy, physical admission, global environment readiness, and quality mode. Ordinary and special tiles retain their full running surfaces, number hierarchy, and eight-player occupancy. Legacy corner-picture planes are removed from the enabled rebuilt course. Full-width special surfaces, continuous edging, directional rail chevrons, hazard trim, and matched portal colors supplement the actual mechanical structures.

Each GLB category loads and retries independently. A temporary silhouette is replaced by the detailed original model when its category loads. Existing URL/content-addressed caches are reused. No new GLB assets or replacement downloads are introduced. Idle authored parts use shared geometry/material instancing; only current event assemblies are articulated separately. Number/caption markings use one atlas draw per map. Economy mode retains every assembly and mesh, reducing shadows and cosmetic score-particle budget (32 full / 16 economy), not categories. This is an implementation budget, not a measured mobile GPU claim.

Display ownership only transfers when a strict physical consumer actually mounts and marks its hardware visible. Actor readiness alone cannot hide a display. Loss of snapshots, reduced motion, or unmount returns ownership and foundation visibility to the display. The registry never grants contact permission.

## Fixed mounts and honest contact limits

Four crowded anchors use a fixed outboard display mount: Wild 23/24 local +Z 1.20 m; Boardwalks 19/23 local +Z 0.45 m. Boardwalks 23 additionally moves local +X 0.70 m (desktop world +Z) to separate its fixed foot and support from 21; its plinth lands at Y=0. Their roots never jump back to the old certified contact mount. Those four anchors explicitly do not borrow old certificates.

- Wild 23/24 use the original R1 mechanism, at a fixed base, with an in-domain 0.22 m spring/glove stroke and a visible targeted air-pressure burst. The 12 previously validated R1 contact trajectories remain historical reference, not current rebuilt-scene contact claims.
- Boardwalks 19/23 keep the original telescopic Trip assembly, with bounded outboard movement and the actual targeted trip/event presentation. Four old desktop/compact banana contact fixtures are excluded at these changed mounts; the other 20 Trip fixtures retain their strict existing gates.
- Other unadmitted characters, crowded cases, and native mechanisms remain complete and articulate cosmetically without forcing unverified contact.
- Wild 16 and 24 have actual ground extensions. Boardwalks 19 has a finite bridge slab with four terrain-clearing posts. Final lamps, stands, terrain, and running-path integration must be checked after merging the separate venue changes.

## Event causality and state

Punch wind originates at the actual authored glove face at peak stroke. Three 3D pressure rings and a short translucent air volume travel to each actual target. A short impact ring is feedback only, not a stun or down status. Movement starts after pressure arrival and follows the authoritative multi-node path, including turns and branches. Only actual targets animate. Seven other occupants, including an already-down racer, retain their positions and down states in the two reviewed eight-player cases. No rule-engine changes are made.

Score flow uses actual positive/negative/zero-clamped deltas and real device outlets. Paired warp endpoints and Trip targets derive from real events. Existing dynamic +1 chips, traps, and trampolines remain governed by actual board state; no new gameplay is introduced.
